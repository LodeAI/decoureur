De Coureur

How it works

Every day there is one cyclist to guess. The same one for everybody, anywhere in the world. At midnight a new one appears.

Eight guesses

You type a rider's name and pick one from the list. After each guess, six columns tell you how close you were.

Guess 3 — Wout van Aert

Country✓Belgium
Age↑29
Team×Visma
Type✓Classics
Turned pro↓2014
Wins↑50+

Read that as: the rider you are looking for is Belgian too and a classics rider too, is older than 29, turned pro after 2014, rides for a different team and won more than fifty.

What the colours mean

  • Green ✓ — exactly right.
  • Gold ~ — close. A neighbouring country, or within three years.
  • Light sand × — no match.
  • ↑ and ↓ — on age, turning pro and wins: higher or lower.

The three colours run from dark to light: green is the darkest, gold sits in between, a miss is the lightest. That difference in brightness holds up in bright sunlight, where colour itself washes out.

Country, Age, Turned pro and Wins can come up gold. Type and Team cannot — they are either right or wrong, with nothing in between.

Every cell carries a mark in its top right corner as well as a colour — ✓, × or an arrow — so the game works if you see colours differently. There is a separate colour-blind mode on top of that.

The six columns

  1. Country — green for the same country, gold for a neighbouring country. Only real land borders count, so Belgium and England are not neighbours.
  2. Age — green within a year, gold within three, with an arrow.
  3. Team — the team the rider is best known for. Right or wrong, with no in-between.
  4. Type — climber, sprinter, classics rider, time trialist, all-rounder. Every rider shows one, and that is what you compare: right or wrong.
  5. Turned pro — the year of the first professional contract, with an arrow.
  6. Wins — career road wins, in bands: 0–9, 10+, 25+, 50+, 99+, with an arrow.

Some days are harder

The week has a rhythm, and it is deliberate:

  • Monday to Thursday — icons of the sport.
  • Friday — an icon too, but always one who has retired. A great name from the past.
  • Saturday — a rider a cycling fan knows.
  • Sunday — one for the connoisseur. Sunday is race day in Flanders, so Sunday is the hard one.

Men and women are mixed throughout. Not a separate category, not a bonus round.

The bidon

Next to the input field there is a water bottle. If you are stuck, it reveals one column value — one you have not turned green yet, because a column you already know is no help.

  • It unlocks after your sixth guess. Not before, and that is deliberate: with two attempts left there is nothing left to drain, and nothing to wait for either.
  • One per day, and you ask for it yourself. It never opens on its own.
  • Free for everyone. The bidon is not behind a subscription.

Whether you used it shows on your result: full and green at zero, empty and red at one. That is what makes "found in four" comparable between two players.

Your streak

Every day you solve extends your streak; skipping a day or failing to find the rider resets it to zero. The flame in the top right of the app shows where you stand.

If you would rather not miss a day, the app sends one reminder per day at a time you choose. Never more than one, never anything else — and it never gives away anything about today's rider.

Sharing

When you finish, the app builds a card to drop into a group chat: your result, your time, your streak, whether you needed a bidon, and your guesses as a grid of coloured squares. It never contains the rider's name, nor their flag — so sharing spoils nothing for anyone who has yet to play.

A text version goes along with it, with the same squares as emoji, for apps that prefer text over an image.

What it costs

The daily puzzle is free and stays free. So are the bidon, the daily reminder, the share card and your streak.

A subscription unlocks the archive of past days, a practice mode and the numbers on how you play: your win ratio, your average number of guesses, the distribution of your guesses, your average time, which generation of riders you find best, and how often you needed a bidon. Never today's puzzle.

What it does not do

  • No account, no login, no registration. The app collects nothing.
  • No ads, no analytics, no trackers.
  • Works completely offline.
