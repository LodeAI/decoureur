De Coureur

How it works

Every day there is one cyclist to guess. The same one for everybody, anywhere in the world. At midnight a new one appears.

Eight guesses

You type a rider's name and pick one from the list. After each guess, six columns tell you how close you were.

Guess 3 — Wout van Aert

Country✓Belgium
Age↑29
Team×Visma
Type✓Classics
Turned pro↓2014
Wins↑50+

Read that as: the rider you are looking for is Belgian too and a classics rider too, is older than 29, turned pro after 2014, rides for a different team and won more than fifty.

What the colours mean

  • Green ✓ — exactly right.
  • Gold ~ — close. A neighbouring country, or within three years.
  • Grey × — no match.
  • ↑ and ↓ — on age, turning pro and wins: higher or lower.

Country, Age, Turned pro and Wins can come up gold. Type and Team cannot — they are either right or wrong, with nothing in between.

Every cell carries a symbol as well as a colour, so the game works if you see colours differently. There is a separate colour-blind mode on top of that.

The six columns

  1. Country — green for the same country, gold for a neighbouring country. Only real land borders count, so Belgium and England are not neighbours.
  2. Age — green within a year, gold within three, with an arrow.
  3. Team — the team the rider is best known for. Right or wrong, with no in-between.
  4. Type — climber, sprinter, classics rider, time trialist, all-rounder. Every rider shows one, and that is what you compare: right or wrong.
  5. Turned pro — the year of the first professional contract, with an arrow.
  6. Wins — career road wins, in bands: 0–9, 10+, 25+, 50+, 99+, with an arrow.

Some days are harder

The week has a rhythm, and it is deliberate:

  • Monday to Thursday — riders anyone who follows the news in July would know.
  • Friday and Saturday — riders a cycling fan knows.
  • Sunday — one for the connoisseur. Sunday is race day in Flanders, so Sunday is the hard one.

Men and women are mixed throughout. Not a separate category, not a bonus round.

Sharing

When you finish you get a small grid of coloured squares to paste into a group chat. It never contains the rider's name, so sharing your result spoils nothing.

What it costs

The daily puzzle is free and stays free. A subscription unlocks the archive of past days, a practice mode and detailed statistics — it never unlocks today's puzzle.

What it does not do

  • No account, no login, no registration. The app collects nothing.
  • No ads, no analytics, no trackers.
  • Works completely offline.

A game about cycling. Not affiliated with the UCI, any team or any race organiser.
